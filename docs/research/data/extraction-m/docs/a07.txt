It's generally **safe to eat eggs past their "sell by" or "best by" date**, provided they have been properly refrigerated and pass a few simple checks.

Here's a breakdown:

1.  **"Sell By" vs. "Expiration" Dates:**
    *   Most eggs have a "sell by" or "best by" date, not a strict "expiration date."
    *   The **"sell by" date** is a guide for retailers to ensure product turnover and freshness. Eggs are typically good for several weeks *after* this date.
    *   The **"best by" date** indicates when the eggs are expected to be at their peak quality.

2.  **How Long Are They Good For?**
    *   If properly refrigerated at 40°F (4°C) or below, eggs can typically be safely eaten for **3 to 5 weeks after their "sell by" date**.

3.  **How to Tell if an Egg is Still Good (The Tests):**

    *   **The Float Test (Water Test):**
        *   Gently place the egg in a bowl of water.
        *   **Good egg:** Sinks to the bottom and lays on its side.
        *   **Older egg (still good):** Sinks but stands on one end. The air cell has expanded, but it's likely still fine to eat, especially if cooked thoroughly.
        *   **Bad egg:** Floats to the surface. This indicates a very large air cell and likely significant moisture loss and/or spoilage. **Discard these.**

    *   **The Sniff Test (Most Reliable):**
        *   Crack the egg into a clean bowl.
        *   **Good egg:** No strong odor.
        *   **Bad egg:** If it has a sulfuric, rotten, or foul smell, even slightly, it's spoiled. **Discard it immediately.** This is the most accurate way to detect spoilage.

    *   **The Visual Test (After Cracking):**
        *   **Good egg:** The yolk should be bright and round, and the whites should be thick and somewhat gelatinous, holding their shape around the yolk.
        *   **Bad egg:** The yolk might be discolored or flat, and the whites might be watery, cloudy, or have an unusual color (pink, green, iridescent). Look for any signs of mold. **Discard these.**

4.  **The Main Risk: Salmonella**
    *   Spoiled eggs taste and smell terrible, so you're unlikely to eat them.
    *   The primary safety concern with eggs is *Salmonella*, a bacteria that can be present inside or on the shell of an egg, regardless of its age or how it looks/smells.
    *   **Thorough cooking kills Salmonella.** Always cook eggs until the yolk and white are firm. Avoid eating runny yolks, raw eggs, or foods containing raw eggs, especially if you're unsure about the egg's freshness or if you are in a high-risk group.

**Who Should Be Most Cautious?**
Young children, the elderly, pregnant women, and individuals with weakened immune systems should always be more careful with egg consumption. For these groups, it's best to use fresher eggs and always cook them thoroughly.

**In summary:** Yes, you can usually eat eggs past their "sell by" date, but always perform the float, sniff, and visual tests. When in doubt, it's always safest to **throw it out.**
