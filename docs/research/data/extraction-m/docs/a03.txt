Taking ibuprofen (an NSAID - Nonsteroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drug) every day, especially without medical supervision, carries significant risks and is generally not recommended. Chronic daily use can lead to a range of serious side effects that increase in likelihood and severity with prolonged use and higher doses.

Here are the main side effects of taking ibuprofen every day:

1.  **Gastrointestinal (GI) Problems:** This is one of the most common and serious risks. Ibuprofen works by blocking prostaglandins, which are also protective to the stomach lining.
    *   **Stomach upset:** Nausea, indigestion, heartburn, abdominal pain.
    *   **Gastric Ulcers:** Open sores in the stomach or duodenum.
    *   **GI Bleeding:** Ulcers can bleed, leading to black, tarry stools (melena), vomiting blood (hematemesis), or anemia. In severe cases, this can be life-threatening.
    *   **Perforation:** In rare, severe cases, an ulcer can perforate (create a hole) through the stomach or intestinal wall.

2.  **Kidney Damage (Renal Issues):** NSAIDs can restrict blood flow to the kidneys, especially in people who are dehydrated, elderly, or have pre-existing kidney conditions, heart failure, or are taking certain other medications (like diuretics or ACE inhibitors).
    *   **Acute Kidney Injury:** Sudden decrease in kidney function.
    *   **Chronic Kidney Disease:** Long-term damage to the kidneys over time.
    *   **Fluid Retention & Edema:** Swelling in the ankles, feet, or hands due to the kidneys retaining salt and water. This can also worsen high blood pressure or heart failure.

3.  **Cardiovascular Problems:** Long-term use of NSAIDs, including ibuprofen, can increase the risk of serious cardiovascular events.
    *   **Increased Blood Pressure:** Can make existing hypertension worse or cause it in susceptible individuals.
    *   **Heart Attack (Myocardial Infarction):** Increased risk, especially with higher doses and prolonged use, or in people with pre-existing heart conditions.
    *   **Stroke:** Increased risk due to blood clot formation or changes in blood pressure.
    *   **Worsening of Heart Failure:** Due to fluid retention.

4.  **Liver Damage:** While less common than GI or kidney issues, prolonged or high-dose use of ibuprofen can sometimes cause liver inflammation or damage, especially in individuals with pre-existing liver conditions.

5.  **Bleeding and Bruising:** Ibuprofen inhibits platelet aggregation (clotting), which can prolong bleeding time. This means you might bruise more easily or bleed more after a cut or injury. It's particularly risky if you're also taking blood thinners (anticoagulants).

6.  **Medication Overuse Headaches (Rebound Headaches):** Ironically, taking pain relievers like ibuprofen too frequently for headaches can lead to a cycle of chronic daily headaches once the medication wears off.

7.  **Allergic Reactions:** Though not specific to daily use, repeated exposure can still trigger allergic reactions in sensitive individuals, ranging from skin rashes, hives, and itching to more severe reactions like asthma attacks (especially in aspirin-sensitive asthmatics) or anaphylaxis (rare but life-threatening).

**Who is at Higher Risk?**
Certain individuals are at an even higher risk for these side effects, including:
*   Elderly individuals
*   People with a history of ulcers or GI bleeding
*   Those with kidney disease, heart disease, high blood pressure, or liver disease
*   People taking blood thinners, corticosteroids, diuretics, or certain antidepressants
*   Individuals who consume alcohol regularly

**Important Advice:**
If you are experiencing chronic pain or inflammation that seems to require daily ibuprofen, **it is crucial to consult a doctor**. Daily use should always be under medical supervision, and your doctor can assess the risks and benefits, recommend alternative treatments (like different medications, physical therapy, or lifestyle changes), and monitor for potential side effects. Self-medicating with ibuprofen daily is dangerous and should be avoided.
