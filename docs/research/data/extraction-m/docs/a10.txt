For a mild burn (such as a first-degree burn or a minor second-degree burn), here are the best steps to treat it at home:

1. **Cool the burn:** Immediately run cool (not cold) water over the burn for 10 to 15 minutes or apply a clean, cool, wet cloth to reduce heat, pain, and swelling.

2. **Protect the burn:** After cooling, gently pat the area dry with a clean cloth.

3. **Apply a soothing lotion or aloe vera:** Using aloe vera gel or an over-the-counter moisturizing lotion can help soothe the skin and prevent dryness.

4. **Cover the burn:** If necessary, cover the burn with a sterile, non-stick bandage to protect it from infection and friction.

5. **Avoid breaking blisters:** If blisters form, do not pop them as this can increase the risk of infection.

6. **Take pain relief if needed:** Over-the-counter pain relievers like ibuprofen or acetaminophen can help manage pain and inflammation.

7. **Monitor for infection:** Watch for signs such as increased redness, swelling, pus, or worsening pain.

**When to Seek Medical Help:**

- If the burn is larger than 3 inches or on the face, hands, feet, groin, or major joints.
- If you notice signs of infection.
- If the burn is deeper or more severe than you initially thought.
- If pain is not relieved by over-the-counter medication.

Always remember that this advice is for mild burns only. For more severe burns, seek medical attention immediately.
