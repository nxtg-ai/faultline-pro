No, your landlord **cannot** keep your security deposit for "normal wear and tear" in California.

California Civil Code Section 1950.5 specifically states what a landlord can deduct from a security deposit, and normal wear and tear is explicitly *excluded*.

Here's a breakdown:

**What a Landlord *Can* Deduct For:**

*   **Unpaid Rent:** Any rent you still owe.
*   **Cleaning:** To return the property to the same level of cleanliness it was at when you moved in, *minus normal wear and tear*. This means if you leave it excessively dirty, they can charge for cleaning. If you leave it reasonably clean, they cannot charge for basic upkeep.
*   **Repairing Damages:** For damages beyond normal wear and tear.
*   **Restoring or Replacing:** Any part of the premises that has been damaged beyond normal wear and tear.

**What is "Normal Wear and Tear"?**

Normal wear and tear refers to the deterioration that occurs naturally and without negligence, carelessness, accident, or abuse. It's the expected aging and degradation of a property through regular use.

Examples of normal wear and tear:

*   Faded paint or wallpaper
*   Minor scuffs on walls (not large holes or crayon marks)
*   Worn carpet in high-traffic areas (not large stains, burns, or tears)
*   Loose door handles or cabinet hinges
*   Slightly yellowed grout in the bathroom
*   Minor scratches on the floor from furniture
*   Small nail holes from hanging pictures (usually acceptable, but excessive holes might be considered damage)
*   Deterioration of appliance finishes due to age

**What is "Damage" (and what they *can* charge for)?**

Damage goes beyond normal wear and tear and is usually caused by tenant negligence, carelessness, or abuse.

Examples of damage:

*   Large holes in walls
*   Pet stains, odors, or scratches on floors/doors
*   Broken windows or mirrors
*   Missing fixtures or appliances
*   Excessive dirt, grime, or mold from neglect
*   Burn marks on countertops or floors
*   Tears or major stains on carpets
*   Unapproved alterations (e.g., painting a bright color without permission)
*   Crayon marks or permanent marker on walls

**Your Rights in California:**

1.  **21-Day Rule:** Your landlord must return your security deposit, or a detailed itemized statement of deductions (along with receipts or estimates), within 21 calendar days of you moving out.
2.  **Pre-Move-Out Inspection:** You have the right to request an initial inspection of the premises up to two weeks before your tenancy ends. The landlord must notify you of your right to this inspection. During this inspection, the landlord must provide you with an itemized statement of any proposed deductions, allowing you an opportunity to fix the issues yourself before you move out, thereby avoiding deductions from your deposit.
3.  **Receipts/Estimates:** If deductions are made, the landlord must provide copies of invoices or receipts for the work done. If the work hasn't been completed within 21 days, they must provide a good faith estimate.

**What to Do if Your Landlord Deducts for Normal Wear and Tear:**

1.  **Gather Evidence:** The best defense is good documentation.
    *   Take extensive photos and videos of the unit when you move in.
    *   Take extensive photos and videos of the unit when you move out, showing its condition.
    *   Keep copies of your lease agreement, move-in checklist, and any correspondence with your landlord.
2.  **Write a Demand Letter:** If your landlord illegally withholds your deposit, send a formal letter (certified mail, return receipt requested) explaining why you believe the deductions are invalid (citing California Civil Code 1950.5) and demanding the return of your money.
3.  **Small Claims Court:** If the landlord still refuses to return the deposit, you can sue them in small claims court. In California, a landlord who "bad faith" withholds a security deposit can be liable for up to twice the amount of the deposit, in addition to the actual amount wrongfully withheld.

In summary, no, your landlord cannot keep your security deposit for normal wear and tear in California. Be informed about your rights and document everything!
