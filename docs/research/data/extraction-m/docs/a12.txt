Electric cars (EVs) generally have a lower environmental impact than gasoline cars over their entire lifecycle, but the extent of the benefit depends on several factors. Here's a breakdown:

1. **Emissions During Use**:
   - **Electric Cars**: Produce zero tailpipe emissions, which means no exhaust pollutants like CO2, nitrogen oxides, or particulate matter during operation.
   - **Gas Cars**: Emit CO2 and other pollutants continuously while driving, contributing to air pollution and climate change.

2. **Emissions from Electricity Generation**:
   - The environmental benefit of EVs depends on how the electricity they use is generated.
   - In regions with a clean energy grid (hydro, wind, solar, nuclear), EVs have a much lower carbon footprint.
   - In areas reliant on coal or natural gas, emissions are higher, but EVs still often produce fewer emissions than equivalent gas vehicles.

3. **Manufacturing Impact**:
   - EVs, especially their batteries, require mining and processing of materials like lithium, cobalt, and nickel, which can have significant environmental and social impacts.
   - Manufacturing EVs can produce more emissions than gas cars upfront, but these are typically offset over the vehicle’s lifetime through cleaner operation.

4. **Lifecycle Analysis**:
   - Studies show that over their entire lifespan (manufacturing, use, disposal), EVs generally produce fewer greenhouse gases than gas cars.
   - The "break-even" point where an EV becomes cleaner than a gas car often occurs after 1-3 years of driving, depending on electricity sources and driving habits.

5. **Other Environmental Concerns**:
   - Battery recycling and second-life use are improving, helping to reduce environmental impact.
   - EVs reduce urban air pollution, improving local air quality and public health.

**In summary:** Yes, electric cars are typically better for the environment than gasoline cars, particularly as electricity grids become greener and battery technologies improve. However, maximizing their benefits requires clean electricity, responsible mining and recycling practices, and continued advancements in technology.
